# Erica Pilot Playbook: Sales Leader AI Coaching Tool

Date: 2026-06-26
Owner: John / MindVault
Prospect: Erica, Atlanta HVAC sales leader

## 1. The product in one sentence

This is not "AI sales training software."

It is Erica's coaching brain turned into a daily rep operating layer:
- reps log visits and voice notes
- AI extracts what happened
- Erica's sales method shapes the coaching
- reps get better next-step guidance in the field
- Erica gets leverage without repeating herself 40 times

## 2. The positioning for Erica

Use this language.

"You already know how to sell. I am not trying to replace your coaching. I want to bottle your coaching style so every rep gets more of Erica, even when you are not on that door, not on that callback, and not reviewing that note live."

"The goal is not generic motivation. The goal is better field execution, faster ramp-up, stronger objection handling, and more consistent next steps."

"If this works, your process becomes an asset the team can run on, not something trapped in your head."

## 3. What you are asking Erica for

You are not asking for a vague brainstorm.
You are asking for a pilot partnership.

Ask for 3 things:
1. Her coaching knowledge
2. Access to real rep situations and objections
3. Permission to dogfood with a tiny pilot group

Exact ask:

"I want to build the first version with a real sales leader, not in a vacuum. You are the best fit because you already know what good looks like in Atlanta HVAC. I want your help turning that into an AI coaching layer for your team."

## 4. The pilot promise

Keep the promise tight.

Do not promise:
- higher close rate in 7 days
- full LMS / training platform
- AI replacing managers
- perfect roleplay or perfect transcripts

Promise this instead:
- reps get more useful next-step coaching from field notes
- Erica's objection handling and judgment start showing up consistently
- new reps ramp faster because the coaching is explicit
- leadership can review whether the AI advice sounds like Erica or not

## 5. The MVP shape

Phase 1 MVP should do only this:

1. Rep logs visit
2. Rep records or uploads transcript / voice note
3. App extracts factual structure
   - summary
   - interest level
   - objections
   - follow-up items
   - contact info
   - next step
4. Coaching engine uses Erica's knowledge base to create:
   - one coaching card
   - one next action recommendation
   - one objection-handling recommendation when relevant
   - one "why this matters" explanation
5. Erica reviews whether it sounds right

Not in MVP:
- full course library
- quizzes
- full AI avatar roleplay
- auto-dialer
- CRM replacement
- giant dashboard sprawl

## 6. The exact 4-phase build sequence

### Phase 0: Secure pilot buy-in

Goal:
- Erica agrees to help shape it
- Erica understands this is a real build, not a demo theater exercise

Outcome:
- verbal yes to pilot
- 1 pilot use case selected
- 1-3 reps identified for dogfooding

Use case to anchor on:
- after-visit coaching for HVAC D2D reps

Why this use case first:
- reps already leave notes
- objections are frequent and repetitive
- Erica can judge advice quality fast
- easy to compare "generic coaching" vs "Erica-style coaching"

### Phase 1: Extract Erica's coaching brain

Goal:
- capture how she actually coaches

You need 5 buckets of knowledge.

1. Qualification logic
- what makes a lead hot, warm, cool, dead
- what separates ready-to-buy from need-to-buy
- what signals financing friction vs real intent

2. Objection handling
- top 10 objections in Atlanta HVAC
- what reps should say
- what reps should never say
- which objections mean "slow down" vs "press forward"

3. Next-step discipline
- when to set callback
- what exact next step counts as real
- how to avoid fuzzy endings like "I'll check back"

4. Rep evaluation
- what makes a note strong vs weak
- what tells her a rep actually controlled the conversation
- what tells her a rep missed the buying window

5. Market-specific judgment
- what matters in Atlanta HVAC
- ready-to-buy vs need-to-buy homeowner differences
- financing/rebate dynamics
- competitor / neighborhood / seasonality context

### Phase 2: Turn her coaching into a knowledge base

Goal:
- convert raw coaching into reusable system inputs

Create 5 knowledge packs.

Pack 1: Erica Core Sales Philosophy
- how she frames trust
- how she controls the conversation
- what a strong rep sounds like
- what a weak rep sounds like

Pack 2: Atlanta HVAC Buyer Types
- ready-to-buy
- need-to-buy
- install timing
- financing / rebate friction
- urgency triggers

Pack 3: Objection Library
- price
- spouse
- not now
- financing concern
- "need to think about it"
- distrust / credibility
- existing competitor relationship

Pack 4: Next-Step Rules
- what next steps are acceptable
- what next steps are too vague
- how soon follow-up should happen
- what callback language converts best

Pack 5: Offer / Process Context
- what the team actually sells
- what financing / install process looks like
- what can and cannot be promised
- compliance / honesty rules

Optional Pack 6: External Framework
- Straight Line summary
- certainty / tonality / control ideas
- only keep what Erica agrees fits her style
- do not force Belfort language if it feels fake for HVAC reps

Important rule:
External frameworks are seasoning, not the main dish.
Erica's real field method must outrank any book framework.

### Phase 3: Pilot the coaching engine

Goal:
- test whether the AI advice sounds like Erica and helps reps

Pilot workflow:
1. 1-3 reps use the app normally
2. reps log notes / transcripts
3. AI produces coaching cards
4. Erica reviews a sample of cards
5. each card gets one of 3 labels:
   - nailed it
   - directionally right
   - wrong / not how I would coach it
6. revise the knowledge base and prompts weekly

### Phase 4: Decide if it is real

At the end of 2 weeks, review:
- did the coaching sound like Erica
- did reps actually use it
- did it improve next-step clarity
- did it help new or weaker reps more than generic coaching

If yes:
- expand to more reps
- add roleplay later
- add manager dashboard later

If no:
- find whether the problem was
  - poor transcripts
  - weak knowledge capture
  - weak prompt composition
  - wrong use case

## 7. The exact discovery meeting with Erica

Run this as a 45-minute working session.

### 0-5 min: frame the opportunity

Say:
"You already have a coaching style that works. I want to turn that into a system your reps can feel every day, especially after field visits and callbacks."

### 5-15 min: isolate the highest-value coaching moment

Ask:
1. "Where do reps need your brain the most right now?"
2. "What do weaker reps consistently miss after a visit?"
3. "What kind of note makes you think, this rep had the lead but lost control?"
4. "If I could make the AI coach one thing well first, what should it be?"

Your target answer:
- after-visit next-step coaching
- objection handling
- buyer-type recognition

### 15-30 min: capture her method

Ask:
1. "How do you know if a homeowner is really ready to buy versus just being polite?"
2. "What are the top objections your reps hear every week?"
3. "What would you tell a rep right after this kind of visit?"
4. "What do you wish every rep wrote down, every single time?"
5. "What advice do you repeat so often that you are sick of saying it?"
6. "What does a great next step sound like, word for word?"

### 30-40 min: define the pilot

Ask:
1. "Which 1-3 reps would give us the cleanest test?"
2. "What would make you say this is actually useful in two weeks?"
3. "What would make you say this is garbage?"

### 40-45 min: close with next action

Say:
"Perfect. I am going to turn this into the first coaching profile, load it into the system, and bring you a small live pilot. Then we will compare the AI coaching against how you would coach the same situation."

## 9. How to structure the knowledge base

Use this exact format for each knowledge document.

### Document template
- Title
- Source owner
- Why this matters
- Rules
- Good examples
- Bad examples
- Trigger phrases
- What the AI should do
- What the AI should never do

### Example
Title: Spouse objection handling
Source owner: Erica
Why this matters: reps lose momentum when they accept a vague spouse objection without securing a specific next step
Rules:
- validate without folding
- do not argue with the spouse concern
- move toward a scheduled follow-up
Good examples:
- "Makes sense. When would be best for both of you to look at it together?"
Bad examples:
- "Okay, just call me whenever"
Trigger phrases:
- "I need to talk to my spouse"
- "I can't decide alone"
AI should do:
- recommend a specific callback or shared review step
AI should never do:
- classify this as dead with no follow-up attempt

## 10. How to judge if the coaching is good

Use a simple scorecard.

Every coaching card should be rated 1-5 on:
1. Fact accuracy
2. Sounds like Erica
3. Usefulness to the rep
4. Quality of next-step advice
5. Quality of objection handling

Add one binary question:
- "Would you actually want a rep to follow this advice? yes/no"

That binary question matters most.

## 11. Success metrics for the first pilot

Do not chase vanity metrics.

Track these 5:
1. % of coaching cards Erica marks as usable
2. % of cards that include a specific next step
3. % of vague notes where AI still produces a helpful recommendation
4. time saved for Erica in repetitive coaching
5. rep adoption: do reps open and act on the cards

Best early win:
- Erica says, "That is basically what I would have told them."
